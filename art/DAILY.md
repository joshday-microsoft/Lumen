| 2026-07-30 | hourglass | loop | one big hourglass filling the panel, walnut caps and posts, glass bulbs, warm haze pooled at the neck. The top bulb drains: the surface dips into a funnel as it goes, a two-pixel stream shimmers down the throat, and a mound builds below with a peak under the fall. When the last grain lands the whole thing flips end-over-end — squash, a lit edge-on bar, squash back — and it is full again. The flip is what closes the loop with no cut: everything drawn is mirror-symmetric about y=15.5, so the drained state flipped IS frame 0, asserted pixel-for-pixel at build time (getting there meant the surface highlight only fires where sand meets air inside its OWN bulb — a bulb filled to the glass has no free surface). 21 frames at 170ms, 7905 bytes; 64 colors compressed smallest again, and banding the radial background haze into three flat steps saved 2.2 KB on its own. Shipped broken the first time: the lower bulb's fill bisection had its invariant flipped (its pixel count DECREASES as the level moves down the panel, same as the top bulb, and I updated the wrong bound), which pinned the bottom completely full in every frame — so it played as a permanently-full hourglass whose top magically refills. The contact strip did not catch it, because gold in both bulbs looks plausible. The build now prints and asserts the fill fraction of each bulb per frame and that neither one ever runs backwards — check the numbers, not the vibe — patient/meditative |

| 2026-08-03 | crane | still | one big origami crane standing on a table, filling the panel: keel body, neck rising to a bent head and a sharp beak, a tail blade swept up-right, the far wing behind and the near wing spread in front. First folded-paper subject in the ledger, and deliberately motionless and near-monochrome after a run of colour and animation. Every part is ONE polygon with ONE fold line: the fold splits it into a lit facet and a shaded facet and is then scored a shade darker — that is the entire lighting model, because at 32px paper reads as paper when adjacent facets of one object differ in VALUE, not when they have gradients. Fold sides are resolved against a reference point known to lie on the lit facet, so the cross-product sign convention never has to be reasoned about per part. Three things it needed: the far wing had to drop a whole value step below everything else (its own darker pair) because it crosses the same corner of the panel as the tail blade and at equal value the bird grew a third wing; the keel was one mushy triangle until it carried the body's centre fold down through it and got a second crease scored across its top; and the wall was left cool while the table was warmed to a plum-grey, so white paper sits between two temperatures and reads whiter than either. Delivered via /paint (1,960 strokes at 16ms ≈ 31s, order = the folding: ground, body, neck, head, beak, tail, far wing, near wing spreading last, then a final pass that scores every crease and catches every lit edge) because this unit's /image path renders blank. Canvas mirror came back pixel-identical to the design — quiet/precise |
