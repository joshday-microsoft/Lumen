Every part is ONE polygon with ONE fold line: the fold splits it into a lit facet and a shaded facet and is then scored a shade darker — that is the entire lighting model, because at 32px paper reads as paper when adjacent facets of one object differ in VALUE, not when they have gradients. The turn is real geometry, not a colour permutation: every top-layer sticker is a quad rotated about the vertical axis by a running angle, so 4 x 90 degrees IS the starting cube (asserted pixel-for-pixel, which also proves the schedule sums to a whole turn). One corner tile on top is amber instead of white — on a solved cube a top-layer turn is invisible from above, and that single odd tile is what makes the rotation read on the face you see most; it laps the cube once per loop while the side colours cycle cyan -> lime -> violet -> magenta as the back faces come round. 16 frames at 130ms, 128 colours, 7723 bytes. Three things it taught: (1) the camera cannot sit at azimuth 45 — the cube is then symmetric about the screen, so a layer 45 degrees into its turn goes perfectly axis-aligned, its top face projects to a plain rectangle, and the whole layer reads as a white card hovering over the cube; at azimuth 34 no sampled angle lands in that pose. (2) At 32px a cubie face is ~7px, so the plastic gap between tiles is UNDER one pixel and can only exist as a TONAL line. Both "crisper" routes destroy it — SS=1 breaks every gap into dashes, and SS=2 snapped back to the flat painted palette biases edge pixels to the dark plastic and turns the tiles into loose chiclets. Keeping the blend costs ~2KB of LZW, which is why this loop buys the bytes back in frames (three motion frames per turn, not six). Corollary: pick the palette on MEASURED quantisation error, not file size — 16 colours is the smallest file AND the one that rounds the grid lines away. (3) The closing frame missed by 21 pixels because radians(360) carries a 2.4e-16 sine: at theta=0 the tile edges land exactly on supersample boundaries, where an infinitesimal nudge flips whole subpixel columns in or out of the raster.
